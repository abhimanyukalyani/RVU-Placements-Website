The numbers

How we report

What each figure counts, who is in the base, when it was recorded, and where our reporting falls short of the standard we have chosen to follow. This page exists so the numbers can be checked rather than trusted.

Disclosure

A standard we are not required to follow

RV University is not a business school and is not required to report placement outcomes to any external standard. There is no regulator asking for this page.

We report on the pattern set by the Indian Placement Reporting Standards — the IPRS — voluntarily, because it is the most demanding disclosure pattern in common use in India and because a figure published without its base, its date and its definitions is not a figure a reader can do anything with.

Following the pattern voluntarily also means we can be honest about where we do not yet meet it. The deviations table below is populated, not decorative.

Definitions

What each term counts

Who counts as seeking placement
A graduating student registered with the placement office for the current cycle who is applying to drives. A student who has not registered is not counted as seeking, and appears in one of the other groups instead. Nobody is counted in two groups, and nobody is left out.
Basic salary, and what it excludes
The fixed annual cash component, before any bonus, equity, joining payment or reimbursement. It excludes the employer's provident fund contribution and insurance. It is the narrowest of the three measures we record, and the one least open to inflation.
Guaranteed cash, and maximum earning potential
Guaranteed cash adds any contractually guaranteed payment to basic salary — a fixed bonus, a retention payment. Maximum earning potential adds everything an offer could pay if every variable component were achieved in full. It is the largest figure and the least likely to be received, which is why it never leads.
How cost to company is composed
Cost to company — CTC — is everything the employer spends on an employee in a year, including non-cash components. It is the figure most often quoted in Indian placement reporting and the least comparable between employers, because what is folded into it varies. We record it, but we lead with basic salary.
One-time joining benefits
Signing bonuses, relocation payments and similar one-time amounts are excluded from basic salary and from guaranteed cash. They are counted only in maximum earning potential, and only where the offer letter states them.
Record date and publication date
The record date is when the count was taken: three months after graduation. The publication date is when it appeared here: six months after. The gap is the time spent reconciling the sheet. Both dates are stamped on every figure so a reader can tell how old a number is.
Offers outside India
Converted to United States dollars and then adjusted for purchasing power using World Bank factors, so that an offer in Singapore and an offer in Bengaluru can sit in the same distribution without one distorting it.

Scope

Which schools are counted, and which are not

RV University has eight schools. Six are in the graduating placement cohort; two are not. Both exclusions are stated here because a school that is silently missing from a placement page is indistinguishable from one that did badly — and quiet exclusion is the specific criticism our audit makes of otherwise strong reporting elsewhere.

No figure anywhere on this site includes a student from an excluded school, in the numerator or in the denominator. The graduating class the percentages are taken against is the six in-cohort schools only, and it is the same number on every page.

When the School of Allied and Healthcare Professions has its first graduating cohort, it enters the figures and this paragraph changes. Continuing education at SCEPS is a different population — working professionals, not graduating students — and reporting it inside the same percentage would make both numbers meaningless.

The base of every figure

Cohort classification

Every graduate appears in exactly one group, and the groups are summed here against the graduating class. The sum is computed when the page loads, so this is a check you are watching happen, not a claim.

Timing

When each thing happens

  • Recorded three months after graduation, which is the IPRS record date and late enough for the cycle to have settled.
  • Published six months after graduation, once the sheet has been reconciled against offer letters.
  • Raw data retained for twelve months, so any figure here can be re-derived from source if it is questioned.

How this site is built

Why some pages need JavaScript, and why that changes in production

Every figure on this site resolves from a single data file at the moment a page opens. No number is typed into any page. That is what lets one correction update the whole site, and it is why every figure can carry the date it was recorded.

The cost is that with JavaScript disabled, the figures do not appear. Each interactive module carries a notice saying so and pointing here or to the office. Those notices deliberately do not repeat the figures: a static copy of a number is a second source, and a second source is the thing that goes stale and starts contradicting the first. Given the choice between a fallback that can drift and no fallback at all, we took no fallback.

This constraint is an artefact of the static build, not of the design. In production on rvu.edu.in the same figures render server-side from Advanced Custom Fields in WordPress, so the markup arrives with the numbers already in it. The no-JavaScript fallback and the search-indexing concern both resolve at that point, and the data spine does not change: the fields become the single source instead of the data files.
