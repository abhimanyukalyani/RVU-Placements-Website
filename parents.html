For parents

Three questions, answered plainly, with no abbreviation left unexplained. Then three things you can actually do — because you are more useful to your child than a reassuring photograph is.

What support does RV University give my child?

RV University runs a placement office — Corporate & Alumni Relations — whose job is to bring employers to campus and prepare students to meet them. In practice that means four things, across all four years of a degree.

They help your child work out what they want. A strengths and interests session in the first year, and sector talks after that. Most students arrive without a clear idea of what work in their field looks like, and this is the part that fixes it.

They prepare the application. A curriculum vitae — a CV, the document listing education and experience that employers ask for — is reviewed and returned within three working days. Mock interviews are run with written feedback.

They bring the employers. Each employer runs a drive: a hiring round on campus where they announce roles, students apply, and interviews and offers follow over a few weeks. Every current drive is published on the live drives page, with its dates.

They apply one rule that protects everyone. One offer at a time. When a student accepts, they step out of the pool so the next student gets the seat. Declining an offer costs a student nothing and is not held against them.

How can I help?

Three things, each with a real route behind it. None of them involves contacting your child’s lecturers.

Refer a recruiter

If you know an organisation that hires people with your child’s skills, tell us and we will approach them. This is the single most useful thing a parent does — most employers arrive through someone who already knew the university.

Offer an informational interview

Thirty minutes with a student in your field, telling them what the work is actually like. You do not need to be hiring, and you are not committing to anything beyond the half hour.

What do the numbers mean?

Placement figures are quoted in a shorthand that is genuinely confusing if you have not met it. Here is the whole of it.

LPA means lakh per annum — how yearly pay is quoted in India. One lakh is one hundred thousand rupees. So a package quoted as 9.4 LPA is a salary of about nine lakh forty thousand rupees for a year of work.

The median is the middle offer. Line every offer up from smallest to largest, and the median is the one in the middle: half the class received more, half less. We lead with the median rather than the highest offer because one exceptional offer tells you nothing about your child’s likely outcome. The highest offer above is shown deliberately smaller than the median, for the same reason.

Every percentage here comes with the number it is out of. A figure like “100% placed” means nothing on its own — a hundred percent of two students is not the same claim as a hundred percent of three hundred. That number underneath is called the denominator, and this site is built so that a percentage cannot be published without it.

Every figure carries a date. The cohort year says which graduating group it describes, and the recorded date says when the count was taken — three months after graduation. Almost no Indian university publishes that date. It is the difference between a number you can check and a number you have to trust.

The full breakdown is on the outcomes page, including what the students who did not seek placement went on to do. The definitions behind every figure are on how we report.

What placement does not guarantee

Registering with the placement office does not guarantee your child a job. No university can promise that, and any that implies otherwise is selling something.

Specifically: not every student who wants a placement receives an offer in the cycle. Not every offer is in the field the student trained for. The figures on this site describe last year’s cohort in last year’s market, and a market can change in a year. A student who skips the readiness sessions and applies to nothing will usually not be placed. The office can only reach a student who answers.

What the office does guarantee is process. Every drive is published to the students it is open to, and the criteria are the same for everyone. Your child can find out where they stand at any point with the eligibility check, rather than by asking a favour.

Who to contact

A role that answers, not a callback form

Corporate & Alumni Relations maintains everything on this site and answers parents directly. Write to placements@rvu.edu.in — that address reaches the office, and a reply comes from the role that handles your question rather than from a ticket queue.

If your question is about your child’s eligibility or applications, say which school they are in and it goes to that school’s placement coordination. If it is about a company you could introduce, it goes to corporate relations. The office page lists the three roles and what each one handles.
